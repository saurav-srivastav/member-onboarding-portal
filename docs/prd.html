Member Onboarding Portal

A single shared workspace that takes a prospective member from first document to go-live — replacing the email threads between the member, Operations, the KYC vendor and Compliance.

Author: Saurav Srivastava Date: 27 Aug 2026 Status: Draft v1

1Problem

Onboarding a new member today runs on email. The member sends documents to Operations; Operations forwards them to the KYC vendor; the vendor's queries travel back through Operations to the member; Compliance weighs in near the end. Each hand-off is a thread, and nobody — including the member — can see the whole picture at once.

The consequences are predictable:

  • Slow. Time-to-onboard is dominated not by review work but by waiting — for a reply, for a missing attachment, for someone to notice a thread has stalled.
  • Opaque. The member's only way to learn their status is to ask. Internally, Service Management can't answer "when will they be live?" without polling Ops.
  • Repetitive. The same clarification ("wrong format", "statement out of date", "signatory missing") is typed fresh into email every time, and resubmissions restart the loop.
Core metric: time taken to onboard a member — measured from application created to member live, with time-in-stage tracked per hand-off so the bottleneck is visible, not guessed.

2Objectives

  1. Transparency. One status timeline, visible to the member and to every internal team, that always answers "where are we, who holds the next action, and since when?"
  2. Fewer round-trips. Catch problems at the point of upload (format, completeness) instead of days later by email; when a human query is unavoidable, attach it to the specific document so one reply resolves it.
  3. Measurability. Every stage transition is timestamped, so the core metric and its per-stage breakdown fall out of normal use — no separate reporting effort.

4The onboarding pipeline

The application moves through explicit states. Every state has exactly one owning team, so "who holds the next action" is never ambiguous, and time-in-state is attributable.

Operations is the single channel to the member and to the vendor. The KYC vendor talks only to Ops; Compliance reaches the member only through Ops. The portal preserves this rule — vendor and Compliance queries land in the Ops queue, and Ops routes them to the member as clarifications — while keeping every hop visible and timestamped, so the routing adds transparency rather than delay.

Invited Service Mgmt

Sales creates the application and invites the member. Clock starts.

Documents in progress Member

Member fills the application form in the portal and works through the supporting-document checklist. Format and size are validated at upload, so a wrong-format file never enters the process. Member submits when both are complete.

Ops review Operations

Ops accepts or returns each document. A return names the specific document and reason, and moves only that item back to the member — the loop is per-document, not per-application.

With KYC vendor Operations · Vendor

Ops dispatches the accepted pack. Vendor queries come to Ops, are logged against the application, and Ops routes them to the member as clarifications; the vendor's final result is recorded by Ops.

Compliance review Compliance

Compliance sees the pack, the KYC result and the full query history, and records approve / reject with rationale. If Compliance needs more from the member, it raises a request that Ops routes onward. Rejection ends the pipeline with a documented reason.

Provisioning Technology

Approval automatically opens the provisioning task: connectivity, accounts, access.

Live Done

Member is onboarded. Clock stops; the application's per-stage timings feed the metrics view.

5Requirements — Phase 1

5.1 Application form & document checklist checklist exists as demo

  • The membership application form is filled directly in the portal as a structured web form — company details, membership class, licence, contacts, declaration — not downloaded and re-uploaded as a PDF. Drafts save automatically, and structured answers can be validated field-by-field and reused downstream (Ops review, the KYC pack, Compliance) without re-keying.
  • Supporting documents (certificates, financials, policies…) are file uploads: named items with plain-language descriptions and accepted formats; upload validated on extension and size, wrong formats rejected with the expected format named.
  • Replace and remove before submission; progress bar of provided vs. required.
  • An explicit Review & submit step once form and uploads are complete — submission, not upload, is the hand-off to Ops; nothing reaches the exchange before it.

5.2 Status timeline

  • The pipeline of §4 rendered as a timeline on every application: completed, current and upcoming stages, who owns the current one, and how long it has been there.
  • Identical truth for member and internal views; the member sees the stage timeline plus the accept/return status of each of their own documents, and internal users additionally see the full clarification and query history.

5.3 Clarifications (kills the email back-and-forth)

  • Clarifications to the member are always issued by Operations — Ops raises its own, and relays those from the KYC vendor or Compliance, which land in the Ops queue as route-onward tasks. The member answers by replying or re-uploading, which resolves the item; the originator sees the answer without another email hop.
  • Open clarifications block stage progression and are visible on the timeline, so a stalled application shows why it is stalled.
  • Common return reasons offered as one-click templates (wrong format, outdated statement, missing signatory…).

5.4 Role dashboards

  • Ops queue: all applications, ordered by time waiting on Ops, with per-document review actions and vendor dispatch.
  • Compliance queue: applications awaiting decision, each opening the single-page review described in §3.
  • Pipeline view (Service Mgmt / management): every in-flight application as a row against the stage pipeline, stalls highlighted.

5.5 Notifications

  • Email notification on stage change and on new clarification, linking into the portal — email becomes the doorbell, never the workspace.
  • Reminder to the stage owner when an application sits in one stage beyond that stage's SLA. Thresholds are per stage (volumes are low, and stages differ widely in expected duration — e.g. Ops review days, KYC screening weeks), kept in a simple admin-editable table.

6Out of scope for Phase 1

  • KYC-vendor API integration — planned as a second step, but longer-term and dependent on vendor team availability. In version 1 the vendor keeps its existing channel and Ops proxies.
  • SSO / corporate identity integration; Phase 1 uses simple invited accounts.
  • E-signatures, document content extraction, or automated screening.
  • Multiple concurrent applications per member firm.
  • Data residency and retention handling for uploaded KYC documents — a production concern to be settled before go-live; the prototype stores files locally with no retention logic.

7Working assumptions

Made to keep version 1 moving; each is cheap to revisit if it turns out wrong.

  1. One document checklist for all membership classes. Class-specific documents can be added later as extra checklist items without structural change.
  2. The KYC vendor keeps its existing channel with Ops in version 1. API integration is the second step, planned longer-term against vendor team availability — the portal's data model records dispatches, queries and results from day one so the integration slots in without rework.
  3. Stall thresholds are per stage, reflecting low onboarding volumes and stages of very different expected duration.
  4. Data residency and retention are deferred to productionisation (see §6); nothing in the prototype should be treated as the system of record.
